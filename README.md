# SQL Product Analytics — D2C Store Case Study

Product analytics on a sample e-commerce store (2,000 customers, 434 orders, ~$106K revenue). Five analytical queries covering revenue trends, acquisition channels, funnel conversion, cohort retention, and product performance — the same questions I'd ask in a PM/product-analyst role.

> The dataset is **synthetic** (generated by `seed.py` with a fixed seed) — the SQL and the analytical thinking are the point.

## Setup

```bash
python seed.py          # builds store.db (deterministic)
sqlite3 store.db < queries/01_monthly_revenue.sql
```

## Findings

**Acquisition channels** (`02_channel_performance.sql`)
- Organic is the best channel: most customers (602), highest revenue ($34.8K), $57.84/customer
- Paid search converts nearly as well (17.5%) at $59.34/customer — worth scaling
- Referral brings the fewest customers and the lowest revenue per customer ($40.40)

**Funnel** (`03_funnel.sql`)
- 30.3% overall view→purchase conversion; the biggest leak is view→cart (55.6%)
- Once a customer reaches checkout, 82.9% complete the purchase — checkout friction is low

**Cohorts** (`04_cohort_retention.sql`)
- Monthly cohorts show repeat-purchase behavior; early cohorts have the most complete picture (later cohorts are right-censored — less time to reorder)

**Products** (`05_top_products.sql`)
- The Basecamp Tent 2P drives 35% of revenue despite mid-pack unit sales — price matters more than volume here
- Outdoor category dominates revenue; Home goods sell units but little revenue

## Files

- `seed.py` — synthetic data generator (fixed seed, reproducible)
- `store.db` — generated SQLite database (rebuild with `seed.py`)
- `queries/` — five numbered analysis queries
